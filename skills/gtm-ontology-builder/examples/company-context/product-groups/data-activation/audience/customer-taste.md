## Emotional register

For DA-1, the proposed starting point is caution about production ownership and
failure handling. Lead with bounded architecture and recovery, then show the
relevant mechanism. This is an illustrative attitude, not observed distrust.

For DA-2, recognize waiting and dependence on another team's capacity, then show
controlled ownership of one audience workflow. Do not imply the role wants to
bypass governance or that frustration establishes purchase intent.

## Content triggers

For DA-1, propose a failure-and-recovery walkthrough with explicit destination
constraints and operator responsibility. For DA-2, propose a before-and-after
workflow showing where a definition is reviewed and approved. These hypotheses
require actual audience response; interest in buying proof alone does not establish
attention, comments, or shares.

## Anti-triggers

For DA-1, unspecified identity behavior and claims of maintenance-free activation
are proposed trust risks. For DA-2, a promise of independence that conceals required
technical ownership is a proposed trust risk. Neither concern justifies removing
the other's responsibilities or replacing the group's production-readiness criteria.

- DA-1: a failed delivery requires finding the operator and checking recovery steps.
- DA-2: an audience change waits in an engineering queue despite an agreed definition.
- DA-2: destination counts need review before publishing an approved audience.
